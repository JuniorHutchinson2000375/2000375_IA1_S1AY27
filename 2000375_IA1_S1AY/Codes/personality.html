LLMs: A Tool, Not a Crutch, in My Academic Life

Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot are becoming common tools for students. To me, the statement that these tools must be used as a tool and not a crutch means that they should help me learn, rather than do all my academic work for me. Technology can make studying easier, but I must still be willing to think, research, practise, and understand the information for myself.

As a university student, I can use LLMs in several ways to improve my learning. For example, when I have difficulty understanding a topic from a lecture, I can ask an AI tool to explain it in simpler terms or provide examples. I can also use it to create practice questions, organise revision notes, and identify areas that I need to study more. When working on assignments, AI can help me brainstorm ideas and develop an outline. However, I should still conduct my own research, use reliable sources, and express my understanding in my own words.

Although these tools can be helpful, they also have disadvantages. AI-generated information is not always accurate, and some answers may contain incorrect facts or references. UNESCO (2023) highlights the importance of using generative AI responsibly and protecting human judgement in education. Therefore, I should verify information using academic articles, textbooks, and other trustworthy sources instead of accepting every answer an AI tool provides.

Another concern is becoming too dependent on AI. If I allow it to complete every assignment, I may receive a good-looking piece of work without actually learning the material. This could affect my critical thinking, problem-solving, and ability to explain concepts independently during examinations or presentations. The U.S. Department of Education (2023) also discusses the need to use AI in ways that support human learning and decision-making.

I believe LLMs are most useful before, during, and after studying. Before a class, I can use them to become familiar with an unfamiliar topic. After a lecture, I can ask questions about concepts I do not understand and test my knowledge with practice exercises. While preparing assignments, I can use AI for guidance, but I must follow my lecturer’s rules and acknowledge its use when required. I should also avoid entering private information or confidential university material into these platforms.

In conclusion, using LLMs as a tool means taking advantage of their benefits while remaining responsible for my own education. A crutch supports someone who has difficulty standing independently; similarly, excessive dependence on AI can prevent a student from developing important skills. My goal should be to use these technologies to strengthen my understanding, not replace my effort. Ultimately, the real value of education is not simply completing an assignment, but gaining the knowledge and skills to apply what I have learned independently.
