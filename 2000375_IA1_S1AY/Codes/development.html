Professional Development Reflection

Reflecting on financial literacy and personal growth inspired by George S. Clason’s timeless teachings in The Richest Man in Babylon.

I discovered from reading these chapters that anyone may develop the behaviors necessary to become wealthy rather than relying solely on luck.

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

The richest man in Babylon, Arkad, advises 100 men how to avoid having a small pocketbook in the Seven Cures:

  • Start thy purse to fattening: 10% of everything should be saved; for every ten coins, spend just nine. This is the first remedy. I learned from this that I do not have to make a lot of money to begin saving. I am able to save a small amount from what I receive even as a student.
  • Control thy expenditures: Budgeting and expenditure management are the second remedy. I agree with Arkad that unless we manage it, our required spending will always increase to match our income.
  • Make thy gold multiply & Guard thy treasures from loss: The third and fourth remedies deal with increasing the amount of gold and preventing its loss. Because he trusted a bricklayer to purchase gems, Arkad lost his initial savings. It taught me not to pursue quick money schemes or invest in stuff I do not understand. Before making an investment, I should consult financial experts.
  • Own thy home, Insure a future income, & Increase thy ability to earn: Owning a home, making plans for my family and old age, and developing my own abilities to increase my income are the fifth, sixth, and seventh cures. The final one is my favorite since it implies that I must increase my value if I want to earn more money.

The Five Laws of Gold

Through the tale of Nomasir, Arkad's son, the Five Laws of Gold carry on the same message. After giving him a tablet containing five laws and a bag of riches, Arkad banishes him for ten years.

Nomasir first loses all of the gold because he was duped in both a disastrous business deal and a horse racing wager. He sells his robes to make ends meet after becoming bankrupt. Then he recalls the tablet.

The five laws are as follows:

  1. Save 10% of your earnings.
  2. Make your gold work for you.
  3. Ask wise folks for counsel.
  4. Do not invest in things you do not know.
  5. Do not chase impossible profits or put your trust in con artists.

Nomasir got wealthy once more and returned with three bags of gold rather than just one after he eventually obeyed the laws.

Key Takeaways & Future Outlook

The most important lesson I have learned is that wisdom is worth more than actual gold. Nomasir soon lost the gold, but he became wealthy once more because of the tablet's wisdom.

The same lessons are taught in both chapters: prioritize saving, live on less than you make, safeguard your finances, and never stop developing yourself. I will be in a better position in ten years if I start saving 10% of my income now and quit pursuing quick cash.
